Design Philosophy
This is not a combat game. The player's weapon is a voice recorder and a notebook. The horror comes from discovering what happened rather than surviving monsters. Every jump scare is earned — the house escalates deliberately. The player should feel clever and then immediately feel afraid.
The house is the antagonist. Not a ghost. Not a monster. The house itself — its architecture, its memory, its need to be witnessed.


## 2. STORY SYNOPSIS & NARRATIVE STRUCTURE

The Truth
In September 1887, Thomas Ashford murdered his wife Eleanor by pushing her down the main staircase. The official verdict: accidental fall. The real story: Eleanor had discovered Thomas's black ledger — a book recording names and death dates going back centuries — and threatened to expose him. Three days after she confronted him, she was dead.
Eleanor's consciousness was absorbed into the walls of Ashford Manor. Not as a ghost. As testimony. As unfinished record. She has been repeating her final hours in the walls ever since, waiting for someone with the skill to document what happened. That person is Mara.
Mara's Background
Mara Voss was a senior investigative journalist at a national newspaper. She uncovered a major financial fraud — had the sources, the documents, the chain of evidence. Her editor spiked the story under legal pressure. Her sources recanted. Mara refused to retract. She lost her job, her marriage, and her professional reputation in the same year.
She rented Ashford Manor for six months. Official reason: a writing retreat. Real reason: she needed somewhere to disappear. She had no idea what she was walking into.
The Keeper System
Ashford Manor requires a Keeper — someone who maintains the black ledger, documenting names and deaths as the house instructs. Every previous tenant was selected by Martin Caldwell, a solicitor who processes the rental agreements and has been complicit in the system for fifteen years.
The Keepers don't know what they're signing up for. They arrive as damaged people — people who told the truth and lost something for it — and the house selects them. They serve, and they die in the house.
Mara is the 48th Keeper. Her death date is written in the ledger: December 2nd. She has three days.
The Choice
Eleanor needs a witness. The house needs a Keeper. Mara can:
A) Become the Keeper — Stay, maintain the ledger, die here eventually.
B) Publish the story — Release Eleanor's testimony publicly, break the chain. Risky. The house may kill her anyway. But Eleanor says no Keeper has ever tried this.
Both options are playable. The canonical ending is Option B: Mara presses Send on an email with every photograph she has taken as evidence. Whether she survives: unknown. The ledger changes her entry from a death date to Incomplete. Option A is a minimal second ending: the player closes the laptop and signs the black ledger instead — a ninety-second, journal-only epilogue in which the house goes quiet in a different key and the entry reads ‘Mara Voss — Keeper 48.’ Eleanor’s last line: ‘Forty-eight of us now. Someone will come. Someone always comes.’ Option B remains canon for Game 2.
That unresolved status — Incomplete — is the hook into Game 2.

## 4. FULL LOCATION GUIDE — EVERY ROOM

EXTERIOR
The Gravel Drive
The opening shot. The player arrives in daylight, estate agent Price's Vauxhall pulling away. Three iron keys on an oxidized green ring in the player's hand. Thirteen windows facing front — the player can count them. Deliberate asymmetry. Victorian Gothic. Three stories of grey stone. Ivy thick enough on the east wall to look structural.
Ambient: The gravel crunches louder than it should. Wind in the overgrown garden. No birdsong.
The Garden (East)
Overgrown hedges gone wild. Accessible from the kitchen door. Dread object: the conservatory remains — glass structure, half-collapsed, lemon trees visible through cracked panes. This is where Eleanor died. The player never enters it. It is always visible from the garden and always wrong in the light.
GROUND FLOOR
Entrance Hall

Dark wood panelling to head height
Ferns-and-songbirds wallpaper above — Victorian, faded to suggestion
Staircase curves up the right wall
Three framed pictures: two landscapes, one portrait (Eleanor, brass plate scratched through)
Key interaction: UV torch on the portrait's scratched name plate reveals Eleanor's hidden inscription: "He was afraid of my name."
Jump trigger (Act 2): After returning from village, afternoon light falls at an impossible angle. A shadow moves along the wall — stops when looked at directly.
Kitchen
Slate counter (grey-green, cold). 1970s modernisation.
Electric kettle. Cracked mug.
Mara's equipment cases — recording gear, laptop.
Kitchen table — Mara's workspace for the entire game.
Key interaction: Press palm against the kitchen wall. The wall moves. Five seconds in, five seconds out. The game's first confirmation: the house breathes.
Key item: The recorder playback at 2:47:16 — the voice-shaped silence.
Breakfast Room
East-facing windows with view of garden and conservatory.
Abandoned breakfast things — a previous tenant's dishes, never cleared.
Atmospheric: A child's drawing pinned to the back of the door. A house, with thirteen windows carefully counted. A figure in one window. The player is in that window position.
Library
Empty shelves (mostly). Fireplace large enough to stand in.
A shelf of books with blank spines — anonymous testimonies. UV torch reveals names on spines.
One shelf section pulled from wall — behind it: roses-and-thorns wallpaper. Sticky note in Mara's hand: Different pattern. Older. Why here?
Key item: A letter in the fireplace flue — Caldwell's handwriting. A lease agreement. The previous tenant's death date is one week after signing.
Drawing Room
Grand piano against the far wall. Sheet music on the stand — 1880s, handwritten.
Chaise longue. Occasional table.
Atmospheric event (Act 2): Piano plays one chord after finding Eleanor's journal. A single note sustains longer than physics allows. Sheet music has changed — in Eleanor's handwriting: "He used to listen to me play. At the end he stopped coming into this room."
Sitting Room
Two armchairs facing a cold fireplace.
Chess set on side table — game mid-play, never finished.
Victorian medical text — Dr. Whitmore's handwriting in margins.
Key item: Whitmore's marginal notes reference "accumulated emotional material" and "testimonial compression." He knew. He documented it scientifically and said nothing.
Key item: The Whitmore Auscultator — a Victorian brass listening instrument, a modified stethoscope with an unusually wide bell, engraved ‘M.W.’, found in its velvet-lined case beside the medical text. Whitmore built it to listen to the walls. Once equipped it replaces nothing Mara brought with her: it is the house’s own diagnostic tool, and it works. (Replaces the EMF meter throughout — see Section 7.)
Dining Room
Table for twelve. Only one place has been used recently — Mara's.
Mirror at the far end of the room.
Jump scare (Act 3): The mirror. Player’s reflection is one step behind. When player stops, reflection catches up. After 30 seconds — the reflection raises a hand and writes on the glass from inside, in mirror-script, illegible in the room. The player must photograph it: in the camera roll the image resolves the right way round — FINISH IT. The journal auto-pins the photograph to the evidence board.
West Wing Door
Solid oak. No window. Hinges on corridor side — opens outward. Wrong.
On arrival: locked. None of the three original keys fit.
In Act 2: A new key appears on the ring. The door breathes open.
Sense design: Air behind this door smells of roses and organic decay. The silence through the keyhole is absolute — not quiet, but absent. EVP recorder shows activity before the door opens.
WEST WING — GROUND FLOOR
West Wing Corridor
42 feet long. The exterior wall accounts for 28 feet. 14 feet of impossible space.
Four doors on the left. One at the end.
The floorboards here do not creak. Silent under any weight.
Echo quality wrong — sound returns too fast.
Architectural horror: The floor plan auto-draws the discrepancy in red with a question mark. The house is bigger inside than outside.
Room 1 — The Spare Bedroom
Bare mattress. Empty wardrobe — doors drift open after closing.
Water stain on ceiling, extensive, but the room below shows no source.
Room 2 — The Sewing Room
Headless dress form standing centre room, wearing Eleanor’s final, unfinished dress — pins still in the seams.
Thread and fabric laid out mid-project. Sewing box open. Scissors missing.
Jump scare: Player enters, explores, turns to leave — dress form is two feet closer. It moves once per visit, and on each visit the dress is closer to finished: seams closed, pins gone, hem complete. The player never sees either change happen. By the third visit the form is directly behind them when they turn, and follows them to the door in silence. After the player finds Eleanor’s journal, the room is empty — the finished dress laid out on Eleanor’s bed with hospital-corner precision. Mara’s journal: ‘Nobody finished that dress. It finished itself. Or she finished it. I don’t know which is worse.’
Room 3 — The Nursery
Cradle wrapped in protective cloth, never unwrapped.
Rocking horse facing the wall — always facing the wall.
Music box on windowsill — closed, silent.
Atmospheric: Music box opens itself on second visit. Plays four notes, then stops. Same four notes as the drawing room piano chord. Rocking horse now faces the door.
Key item: Inside the cradle wrappings, Eleanor's early journal — 1884-1885. Her engagement to Thomas. Happiness, and no awareness yet of what he is.
Room 4 — Thomas's Study (Initially Locked)
Locked until Act 3. 12 by 15 feet. One east-facing window.
Floor-to-ceiling bookshelves — legal texts, medical journals, county records.
Mahogany desk. Thomas's diary open to the final entry.
Key item: Thomas's diary. "September 14th, 1887. She knows." Two words. Rest of the pages blank — but with texture, as if words were once there and removed.
UV torch reveal: The removed words read: "The book is in the foundations. She will find it. I am sorry. I am not sorry enough."
Key item: The black book on the bottom shelf. Names. Dates. Mara's name. Three days from now.
End Room — Eleanor's Room
The most important room in the game.
Bed made with hospital corners, white coverlet. Washstand — water in the pitcher has not evaporated in 136 years.
Wardrobe slightly open — edge of dark Victorian dress visible.
Writing desk against far window. Papers. A pen laid across them. Inkwell open.
Roses-and-thorns wallpaper, hand-printed.
Entry: Room responds to the player's arrival. EVP recorder lights red, then blue, then amber — she is here and she is aware of you.
Key item: Eleanor's journal — fever-hot when picked up. Screen distortion. The warmth does not go away for the rest of the game.
Key item: Loose paper — ink still wet. "You are the witness. You are the one who will carry this truth into the world. I have been waiting so long."
Key item: The black book in the wardrobe — cold as metal in winter. Last entry: Mara's name. December 2nd.

CELLAR
Cellar Stairs
Stone steps. Twelve of them. Mara counts automatically.
The light beam thins as it descends — the air itself absorbs light.
Old air. Metallic taste.
Cellar Main Space
Packed earth floor, hard as concrete. Foundation stones exposed.
Wooden support beams. Ceiling low — duck slightly.
Sense design: The frequency here is strongest. Felt in the back of the teeth. A pressure. EVP recorder's amber becomes deep orange. Red means: something is using this space as an anchor.

On the stone shelf. Not hidden. Not protected. Waiting.
Contains: A century and a half of keeper accounts. Thomas's entry. Eleanor's three sentences. The 2019 keeper — Helen Whitmore, descendant of Dr. Marcus Whitmore — and her shaky margin note.
Final pages: blank. Waiting for Mara.
The Coal Room
Adjacent. Heavy iron door. Dark, cold, old coal dust smell.
One loose brick. Third row up from the floor, east wall.

A secret space accessed via a gap behind the library shelves. 8 feet wide — only part of the corridor’s 14-foot discrepancy; six feet remain unaccounted for. No windows. No light source except what comes through the gap.
Forty-seven candles — one for each keeper before Mara. All burned to the base.
Forty-seven names scratched into the walls, floor-to-ceiling.
Eleanor's original testimony scratched into plaster in letters half an inch high, every surface.
A single chair in the centre, facing the entrance.
The player cannot enter this room. They can only look through the gap. Eleanor appears here once — at the end of Act 3, turning to look at the player, nodding once. Then the shelves swing back.

Mara's journal: "Not words. The shape of words. Female register. I've noted it and I'm moving on. That's what I do. I note it and I move on."
ACT 2 — INVESTIGATION (0:45–1:45)
The Key Count (0:45–0:50)

Whitmore. The same family name as the previous keeper. Connection flagged automatically in Mara's journal.
The Portrait (1:05–1:10)
Eleanor's portrait examined properly. UV torch on the scratched name plate: He was afraid of my name. Why scratch out a dead woman's name? What could her name do?
The West Wing (1:10–1:35)
The new key. The door. The corridor that is 14 feet too long. Sequential room discoveries:
1. Spare bedroom — wardrobe that won't stay closed

3. Nursery — music box opens, rocking horse has moved
4. Thomas's study — LOCKED. Player notes it and moves on.
5. Eleanor's room — the centrepiece of Act 2, 15–20 minutes here
Eleanor's room discoveries: The journal (haptic feedback — warm). The loose papers (ink still wet). The black book. Their own name. December 2nd. Three days from now.

The Study Opens (1:35–1:45)
Thomas's study is now accessible — unlocked with no explanation given. The house is complicit.
Thomas's diary. The UV reveal of removed words. The full Keeper Chain in the black book. Forty-seven people. All referred by Caldwell's firm. All dead. Among them: James Keller, 12/01. Mara reads it American-style first — December 1st, tomorrow — and panics; then corrects herself. UK format. 12 January. Six weeks. Verify, then conclude. She writes both readings in the journal.
ACT 3 — REVELATION (1:45–2:15)
Martin Caldwell's Visit (1:45–2:00)
A knock at the door. Caldwell — professional, warm, unremarkable. He comes inside. Sits at the kitchen table. The player chooses one of four questions to ask; the remaining three appear afterwards in Mara’s journal under ‘Things I should have asked.’ His tells: hands stop moving when asked about Eleanor; doesn't reach for the black book; "I process rental agreements, Ms. Voss. I don't bring anyone anywhere."
He sees everything on the table. He doesn't panic. He thanks Mara for her time and leaves.
After he leaves — Mara's journal: "He's going to do something. I have maybe hours. Maybe less."
The Cellar (2:00–2:10)
Player descends. Finds the archive book. Reads keeper accounts.
Eleanor speaks — first clear full sentence of the game: "The house doesn't want death. The house wants completion. We're all in the walls because we told the truth and nobody wrote it down. You can write it down. You're the first person who can write it down in a way the world will accept. But the book wants you to stay. The book always wants the Keeper to stay."
The Choice (2:10–2:15)
The player is in Thomas's study. The laptop is open. The email is already drafted — attached: every photograph, every piece of evidence. Subject line: Eleanor Ashford. Ashford House. 1887 to present. Everything.
The player has to actively press Send — or may instead close the laptop and sign the black ledger, taking the Keeper ending (Option A: a ninety-second, journal-only epilogue). Option B is canon for Game 2.
The game pauses. A beat of silence. Then Eleanor's voice, very quiet: "Thank you." The house settles. A pressure releases from every room simultaneously.
ACT 4 — RESOLUTION (2:15–2:30)
The Morning After (2:15–2:20)
Player wakes in Thomas's study. Dawn light. The house is the quietest it has ever been. The front door is open. Martin Caldwell stands in the garden, facing away from the house. He stands for ten minutes. Then walks to his car and drives away without looking back.
Walking the House (2:20–2:25)
Slow atmospheric sequence. Walk through every room. The drawing room piano plays itself — not horror this time — a full piece, from beginning to end.
Eleanor appears in the dining room. Sitting. Not frightening. She is present the way a person is present. She looks up. "It's working. You've made my testimony real. Real things don't need a house to hold them."
The Ledger Entry Changed (2:25–2:28)
The black book. Mara's entry:
"Mara Voss, 31, journalist. Arrived November 28, 2023. December 2nd: Incomplete."
Not a death date. A status. The house has no category for what Mara has done.
Final Shot (2:28–2:30)
The player descends to the cellar one final time. The archive book — open to a new page in Mara's handwriting: "December 2nd, 2023. Eleanor Ashford, witnessed." Below it in the original cramped hand: "The first Keeper who chose truth over survival. The chain changes here."
The player moves to the coal room. The loose brick. Their hand approaches. Temperature changes. The camera holds on the brick for three seconds. Then: a sound. Faint. From behind the brick. Not the house breathing. Something from further away.
Cut to black. Text on screen: The Sealed Universe continues in Game 2: What Breathes in the West

## 7. MECHANICS — INVESTIGATION, GADGETS & INVENTORY

Core Investigation Loop
Step
Action

## 1. EXPLORE

Walk the house. Notice details. Interact with objects.

## 2. RECORD

Place recorders in rooms. Return later. Review audio for EVP events.

## 3. DOCUMENT

Photograph evidence. Collect items. Mara's journal auto-builds from what is examined.

## 4. ANALYSE

UV torch reveals hidden text. The Whitmore Auscultator maps testimony density in the walls and finds hidden spaces.

## 5. CONNECT

Evidence board links clues. Correct connections unlock Eleanor's dialogue, new rooms, new EVP events. Incorrect connections degrade testimony until corrected — the house only cooperates with a truthful witness.
Gadgets
Voice Recorder (Olympus)

## 8. UI DESIGN

Philosophy
The UI is Mara's toolkit. Everything is diegetic where possible — the map is drawn in her notebook, the inventory is her equipment cases, the notes are her journal. No floating health bars. No waypoints. No indicators unless they come from her equipment.

False or sloppy connections cause testimonial degradation — quieter recordings, fragmented journal entries — never damage. Rest: Mara sleeps once (Act 1 to Act 2). After that, she does not sleep. By Act 3 the mirror shows a woman who has been awake too long.


## 9. AUDIO DESIGN

Music
No traditional score. Three persistent audio elements:
The Frequency: A sustained subsonic tone that increases in intensity as supernatural activity increases. Below conscious hearing in quiet sections. Rises to audible in the cellar, Eleanor's room, and during EVP events.
Eleanor's Theme: Four notes. Piano. First heard in the drawing room Act 2. Reappears in the music box (nursery), in the 2:47 recording (barely there), and in full at the resolution.
Thomas's Theme: No melody. A rhythmic pattern — the sound of a pen scratching paper, formalised into a percussive beat underlining his study and diary scenes.

## 11. CONNECTIONS TO GAME 2

Five Unresolved Threads
1. The Loose Brick
The archive book in the cellar is still there. Eleanor said: "Leave it. It doesn't need to be opened. It needs to be witnessed." But Mara's journal entry on the final night reads: "I haven't opened the brick. I should. I know I should. Something is behind it that isn't the book. The book is the visible part. The real record is something else."
2. The Incomplete Entry
Mara's ledger entry reads Incomplete. The house's logic has no category for what she has done. The system continues — just with Mara in an indeterminate state. She is neither a Keeper who has fulfilled her role nor a Keeper who has died. The chain is broken but not ended.
3. The Network
Mara's final journal entry mentions a letter from Edinburgh. A woman named Dara Mackenzie. She maintains a circus archive. She has felt the same frequency. The implication: Ashford Manor is not unique. There are other houses. Other archives. Other Keepers.
4. The Entity in the Book
Eleanor explains the house and book as two parts of a system — the book as visible record, the house as actual archive. But the original text on the study page refers to a library where old records were kept, before the fire. There was something before Ashford Manor. There was a fire. There were records that predate the book by centuries.
5. The West Wing Geometry
14 feet of space that doesn't exist outside. The gap room is inside those 14 feet, but it accounts for only 8 feet of it. Six feet remain unaccounted for. The game never goes there. There is no door.
Game 2 Premise
What Breathes in the West picks up with Mara having stayed in the house for three months after the events of Game 1. She has not left. The six unexplained feet. The fire that predates Thomas. The archive that existed before Ashford. The other Keepers across the world, each maintaining a piece of something that connects to something else.
Game 2 moves into the space behind the space. Into what the west wing is actually connected to. Into the original fire. Into what breathes in the walls — because Eleanor's three sentences in the archive book state clearly: I told the truth. No one believed me. The house believed me.
The house believed her because the house had already been told. Because something had already been there, in that space, receiving testimony, for longer than the house itself has existed.
That entity — patient, accumulating, neither malevolent nor benevolent, simply archival — is the subject of Game 2.
